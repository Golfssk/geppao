# GepPao Business Model v1

Status: product decision for the supply-acquisition stage  
Updated: 22 September 2026

## 1. Product principle

GepPao is a relevance-first local trip planner for Pak Chong and Khao Yai. The product must earn traveler trust before monetizing distribution.

Therefore:

- Business listings, owner onboarding, draft creation, Smart Import, and basic information updates remain free during the supply-acquisition stage.
- Payment never secretly improves AI ranking.
- Planner recommendations prioritize request relevance, data readiness, practical constraints, and transparent uncertainty.
- Sponsored placements, if introduced, must be clearly labeled and visually separated from organic recommendations.
- GepPao does not invent place facts, prices, coordinates, opening hours, contact details, or media rights.

## 2. Who receives value

### Travelers

- Discover useful local places with clearer data confidence.
- Build day trips and overnight trips from real published data.
- Save time while retaining visibility into estimates and missing information.

### Business owners

- Create a free business presence.
- Import public information from a website or Facebook URL into a reviewable Draft.
- Maintain operational facts used by travelers and Planner.
- Gain qualified traveler intent rather than paying merely to exist in search results.

### GepPao

- Builds a differentiated local-data graph.
- Improves Planner quality as owners maintain accurate information.
- Monetizes workflow value and measurable demand—not hidden ranking influence.

## 3. Free core offer

The following stay free initially:

- One Business profile per owner account.
- Place and Event Draft creation.
- Smart Import with source provenance and media-rights confirmation.
- Editing core facts, hours, prices, policies, contact channels, and licensed media.
- Standard publication review.
- Organic discovery and eligibility for relevance-based Planner recommendations.
- Basic traveler actions such as opening maps or contacting the business.

Free access is deliberate: the immediate constraint is data coverage and owner adoption, not payment conversion.

## 4. Paid products to test later

Paid features should save staff time, improve operations, or deliver measurable demand. Candidate products:

### Owner Pro tools

- Multi-location and team permissions.
- Bulk updates for hours, prices, images, and seasonal changes.
- Scheduled updates and expiry reminders.
- Enhanced performance analytics and demand insights.
- Lead-management workflow and export.
- Faster operational support without promising response times before capacity exists.

### Transaction and referral revenue

Only after demand is proven:

- Booking or reservation referrals with a disclosed commission.
- Qualified lead fees where the owner explicitly opts in.
- Event or activity transactions where GepPao can track outcomes reliably.

### Clearly labeled sponsorship

- Sponsored cards or campaign modules may be sold later.
- Every paid placement must display a clear “Sponsored/ผู้สนับสนุน” label.
- Sponsorship must not alter the organic Planner score or masquerade as an AI recommendation.
- Performance reports should separate paid reach from organic relevance.

## 5. Explicit non-models

GepPao will not use:

- Hidden pay-to-rank in Planner or organic discovery.
- Charging owners merely to correct core facts.
- Auto-publishing imported content without owner review.
- Reselling personal data.
- Claiming guaranteed bookings, traffic, or response times without evidence and operational capacity.

## 6. Rollout gates

### Stage A — Supply acquisition (current)

Goal: useful coverage and owner trust.

- Keep owner listing tools free.
- Improve data completeness and media rights.
- Measure owner onboarding completion, Draft completion, moderation readiness, Planner coverage, and traveler engagement.

Do not introduce paid ranking.

### Stage B — Workflow validation

Start only when there are enough active owners and repeated operational use.

Test willingness to pay for one narrow tool, such as multi-location updates, lead workflow, or advanced analytics. Use interviews and a manual concierge pilot before building a full subscription system.

### Stage C — Demand monetization

Start only when GepPao can attribute meaningful traveler actions.

Pilot disclosed referrals, qualified leads, or transactions with a small owner cohort. Preserve the free listing tier.

### Stage D — Sponsorship

Start only after organic ranking is stable and labeling standards are implemented. Sponsored inventory must remain separate from organic Planner output.

## 7. Metrics and decision rules

Track:

- Active owner accounts.
- Onboarding-to-first-Draft conversion.
- Draft-to-published conversion.
- Percentage of published Places that are Planner-ready.
- Owner update frequency and stale-data rate.
- Planner run-to-saved-trip conversion.
- Qualified traveler actions: map opens, contact actions, event interest, and attributable referrals.
- Support workload per active owner.

Do not add a paid plan until interviews show a repeated operational problem and at least a small cohort agrees to pay for the solution. Do not price from page views alone.

## 8. Initial commercial hypothesis

The first paid offer should be an optional **Owner Operations Pro** plan, not a listing fee.

Hypothesis:

- Free: one Business, normal owner tools, organic relevance-based discovery.
- Pro: multi-location/team operations, bulk and scheduled updates, enhanced demand analytics, and lead workflow.
- Referral/transaction fees: separate opt-in commercial agreement.
- Sponsored campaigns: separately labeled and excluded from organic AI ranking.

Pricing remains undecided until owner interviews and usage evidence exist. No price should be published or promised before that validation.

## 9. Next validation actions

1. Recruit 5–10 real local owners into the free flow.
2. Observe onboarding and Smart Import without assisting unless they are blocked.
3. Record the recurring tasks that cost owners the most time.
4. Ask which operational outcome is worth paying for and what they use today.
5. Run one manual paid-feature pilot before implementing billing.
6. Revisit this model using actual conversion, support, and engagement evidence.
